Signals & ideas

Buy more
Pulled back, looks cheap, and shows signs of rebounding: a good time to add.
Hold
Healthy; give it time to reach its next peak before selling.
Risky
Something looks off, so watch closely; selling may be safer, though not necessarily at a good price.
Profit sell
Up well, but the run may turn down soon: a good moment to lock in the gain.
Urgent sell
Serious and time-sensitive: consider selling at the next chance. Also sends a phone alert.
Sentiment
The model's overall read: Bullish (likely up), Neutral, or Bearish (likely down).
Score
0-100: how well a new idea fits the model's checklist; 90+ shows in green.
Swing idea
A stock that dipped in an uptrend or just turned up, likely to rise within days to weeks, with a planned entry, stop, and target.
Long-term idea
A business worth owning for years, judged on valuation, financial health, growth, and moat.

Your portfolio

Prev. day / Day
One full session's change, close to close: the previous session before the open, today's after the close.
Unrealized P/L
Gain or loss versus what you paid on positions you still hold; realized only when you sell.
Weight
A position's share of your portfolio's value.
Flagged
Positions marked Risky, Profit sell, or Urgent sell.
Beta
How much it moves with the S&P 500: 1 moves with it, 2 twice as much, below 1 calmer.
Volatility
How much the price typically swings in a year; higher is bumpier.
ATR
Average True Range: the typical daily price range, as a % of price; useful for setting stops.
Leveraged ETF (2x, 3x)
Multiplies an index's daily move; gains and losses compound, and choppy markets erode it.

Technical indicators

RSI 14
Relative Strength Index, 0-100 momentum over 14 days: above 70 overbought (may pull back), below 30 oversold (may bounce).
EMA 9 / 21
9- and 21-day averages weighted to recent prices; price above the 21 suggests an uptrend, below a downtrend.
50 / 200-day average
Medium- and long-term trends: price above both is healthy; the 50 crossing above the 200 is a bullish 'golden cross'.
Bollinger bands
Two standard deviations either side of the 20-day average: near the top band the price is stretched high, near the bottom, low.
Parabolic SAR
A trailing stop: below the price in an uptrend, above it in a downtrend; a flip signals a possible reversal.
Volume vs 3-month avg
Volume versus normal; well above 1x means unusual interest, giving a move more weight.
52-week range
The year's low and high, and where the price sits between them.
Entry / Stop / Target
The suggested buy price, the price to cut the loss at, and the profit target.
Reward : risk
Potential gain per $1 risked to the stop; 2 : 1 or better is generally good.

Valuation & fundamentals

Market cap
Share price times shares outstanding: the company's total value.
P/E (trailing, forward)
Price over the past (trailing) or next (forward) 12 months' earnings per share; lower can mean cheaper, given peers and growth.
PEG
P/E divided by earnings growth: about 1 is fair, below 1 cheap for its growth.
EPS
Earnings per share: profit divided by shares outstanding.
Revenue / earnings growth YoY
The change versus the same period a year earlier.
Net margin
The share of each sales dollar kept as profit.
ROE
Return on equity: profit per dollar of shareholders' equity; above about 15% is strong.
Debt / equity
Debt relative to shareholders' equity, in %; higher means more leverage and risk.
EBITDA
Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization: rough operating profit.
Analyst rating & target
Wall Street's consensus (Strong buy to Sell) and average 12-month target; the % is the upside from today's price.
Earnings (before open, after close)
When quarterly results come out; prices often move sharply around them.

Market indicators

Signal grades
Each indicator's dot and label: green is good for stocks, yellow a caution, red a warning, grey no clear signal. The key ones show first; the rest are under All indicators.
S&P 500 (SPY)
500 large US companies weighted by size, the market's baseline: above its 50- and 200-day averages is an uptrend, below both a downtrend, between them a pullback or a recovery.
Nasdaq-100 (QQQ)
100 large non-financial Nasdaq companies, tech-heavy and rate-sensitive: it leads rallies and falls fastest, and beating the Dow means investors are taking risk (risk-on).
Dow Jones (DIA)
30 industrial and financial blue chips, weighted by price: they hold up better in tech sell-offs and when rates are high, so beating the Nasdaq-100 means investors are playing defense.
NYSE Composite
Every NYSE stock (2,000+), not skewed by mega-caps: when the S&P 500 beats it, a few giants are carrying the market.
Russell 2000 (IWM)
2,000 small US companies, sensitive to borrowing costs: lagging the S&P warns of tight credit, leading it signals confidence in the economy.
A vs B (pts)
A's move minus B's over 5 days, in percentage points; 2 or more counts as a lead.
Market mood
VIX and Fear & Greed read together: VIX jumps within hours of a shock while Fear & Greed moves over days, so a VIX spike while greed still shows is a fresh shock, and fear on both is capitulation.
VIX
Expected S&P 500 volatility over 30 days, from option prices: under 20 (about its long-run average) calm, 20-30 elevated, over 30 high fear.
Put/call ratio
Cboe's daily put volume over call volume, beside its 5-day average: 0.70-1.00 a normal balance, over 1.00 heavy put buying (fear), under 0.70 heavy call buying (over-exuberance).
Fear & Greed
CNN's 0-100 blend of 7 mood gauges, in CNN's bands: under 25 extreme fear, under 45 fear, up to 55 neutral, up to 75 greed, then extreme greed; the extremes often come near turning points.
S&P breadth
The share of S&P 500 stocks above their 50-day average: over 60% a broad, strong market, under 40% a weak one carried by a few giants.
US Dollar (DXY)
The dollar against major currencies: down 1%+ in 5 days is a tailwind for stocks, up 1.5%+ a headwind for multinationals' earnings and risk assets.
10Y yield
The 10-year Treasury rate, the base cost of capital: up 25+ bps in a month weighs on stock valuations, especially growth stocks.
10Y-2Y spread
The 10-year minus the 2-year Treasury yield: below zero (inverted) has often preceded recessions, and turning positive again has historically come even closer to one.
Crude oil (WTI)
The US oil price, the main energy input: up 10%+ in 5 days feeds inflation; down as much often signals weaker demand.
5D
Over the last 5 trading days.
bps
Basis points: hundredths of a percent, so 100 bps = 1%.
pts
Percentage points: the gap between two percentages, e.g. 50% to 53% is +3 pts.
